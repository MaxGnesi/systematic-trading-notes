# Lecture 25: sources checked at source (2026-11-13)

Checked on RePEc/EconPapers, Crossref, NBER, AQR and UPF pages; SSRN and the JPM publisher site refused automated fetches.

- Berk, J. B. and Green, R. C. (2004). Mutual fund flows and performance in rational markets. *Journal of Political Economy* 112(6), 1269-1295. doi:10.1086/424739. Decreasing returns to scale; "past performance cannot be used to predict future returns". Confirmed: ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/v112y2004i6p1269-1295.html
- Pástor, Ľ., Stambaugh, R. F. and Taylor, L. A. (2015). Scale and skill in active management. *Journal of Financial Economics* 116(1), 23-45. doi:10.1016/j.jfineco.2014.11.008. "strong evidence of decreasing returns at the industry level". Confirmed: RePEc.
- Novy-Marx, R. and Velikov, M. (2016). A taxonomy of anomalies and their trading costs. *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1), 104-147. doi:10.1093/rfs/hhv063. Buy/hold spread; anomalies with < 50% monthly turnover survive costs. Confirmed: EconPapers, NBER w20721.
- Frazzini, A., Israel, R. and Moskowitz, T. J. (2018). Trading costs. Working paper, SSRN 3229719 (AQR). Not published in a journal as far as found. $1.7 trillion of live trades; "actual trading costs to be an order of magnitude smaller than previous studies suggest"; impact concave in size. AQR's own trades. Confirmed: aqr.com working-paper page.
- Jagannathan, R. and Ma, T. (2003). Risk reduction in large portfolios: why imposing the wrong constraints helps. *Journal of Finance* 58(4), 1651-1683. doi:10.1111/1540-6261.00580. No-short constraint acts as shrinkage. Erratum (proof of Prop. 2) on afajof.org; conclusions kept. Confirmed: EconPapers.
- DeMiguel, V., Garlappi, L. and Uppal, R. (2009). Optimal versus naive diversification: how inefficient is the 1/N portfolio strategy? *Review of Financial Studies* 22(5), 1915-1953. doi:10.1093/rfs/hhm075. 14 models, none consistently beats 1/N; ~3000 months needed for 25 assets. Confirmed: RePEc.
- López de Prado, M. (2016). Building diversified portfolios that outperform out of sample. *Journal of Portfolio Management* 42(4), 59-69. doi:10.3905/jpm.2016.42.4.059. HRP. Bibliography confirmed via Crossref; abstract wording NOT confirmed at source (SSRN blocked); author's own Monte Carlo.
- Ledoit, O. and Wolf, M. (2004a). A well-conditioned estimator for large-dimensional covariance matrices. *Journal of Multivariate Analysis* 88(2), 365-411. doi:10.1016/S0047-259X(03)00096-4. Confirmed: RePEc, Crossref.
- Ledoit, O. and Wolf, M. (2004b). Honey, I shrunk the sample covariance matrix. *Journal of Portfolio Management* 30(4), 110-119. doi:10.3905/jpm.2004.110. Confirmed: UPF working paper 691, Crossref.
- Maillard, S., Roncalli, T. and Teïletche, J. (2010). The properties of equally weighted risk contribution portfolios. *Journal of Portfolio Management* 36(4), 60-70. doi:10.3905/jpm.2010.36.4.060. Bibliography confirmed via Crossref; abstract wording (volatility between minimum variance and 1/N) NOT confirmed at source.

Still to confirm in a browser: the abstract wording of López de Prado (2016) and Maillard et al. (2010), and Frazzini et al.'s SSRN page.

## Market selection: clustering, noise, diversification (checked 2026-11-13; Crossref, arXiv, OpenAlex)

- Mantegna, R. N. (1999). Hierarchical structure in financial markets. *European Physical Journal B* 11(1), 193-197. doi:10.1007/s100510050929. MST and hierarchical tree of stocks group by industry. Caveat: the arXiv preprint (cond-mat/9802256) uses d = 1 - rho^2; the sqrt(2(1-rho)) form is attributed to the journal version by secondary sources and used by Onnela et al. (2003): cite it to Onnela et al. or the journal version.
- Onnela, J.-P., Chakraborti, A., Kaski, K., Kertész, J. and Kanto, A. (2003). Dynamics of market correlations: taxonomy and portfolio analysis. *Physical Review E* 68(5), 056110. doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.68.056110. Tree shrinks in crashes; "the assets of the classic Markowitz portfolio are always located on the outer leaves of the tree".
- Tumminello, M., Lillo, F. and Mantegna, R. N. (2010). Correlation, hierarchies, and networks in financial markets. *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization* 75(1), 40-58. doi:10.1016/j.jebo.2010.01.004. Review; clustering as filtering.
- Laloux, L., Cizeau, P., Bouchaud, J.-P. and Potters, M. (1999). Noise dressing of financial correlation matrices. *Physical Review Letters* 83(7), 1467-1470. doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.83.1467.
- Plerou, V., Gopikrishnan, P., Rosenow, B., Amaral, L. A. N., Guhr, T. and Stanley, H. E. (2002). Random matrix approach to cross correlations in financial data. *Physical Review E* 65(6), 066126. doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.65.066126. "a majority of the eigenvalues of C fall within the RMT bounds"; the deviating ones are market and sectors, stable over time.
- Meucci, A. (2009). Managing diversification. *Risk*, 74-79 (volume/issue NOT confirmed; no DOI). Effective number of bets (from secondary sources).
- Rudin, A. M. and Morgan, J. S. (2006). A portfolio diversification index. *Journal of Portfolio Management* 32(2), 81-89. doi:10.3905/jpm.2006.611807. "measures the number of unique investments in a portfolio".
- Raffinot, T. (2017/2018). Hierarchical clustering-based asset allocation. *Journal of Portfolio Management* 44(2), 89-99 (online Dec 2017, issue Winter 2018). doi:10.3905/jpm.2018.44.2.089. Abstract wording NOT confirmed.
- Choueifaty, Y. and Coignard, Y. (2008). Toward maximum diversification. *Journal of Portfolio Management* 35(1), 40-51. doi:10.3905/jpm.2008.35.1.40. Diversification ratio.
- Elton, E. J., Gruber, M. J. and Blake, C. R. (1996). Survivor bias and mutual fund performance. *Review of Financial Studies* 9(4), 1097-1120. doi:10.1093/rfs/9.4.1097. "funds that disappear tend to do so due to poor performance".
- Friedman, J., Hastie, T. and Tibshirani, R. (2008). Sparse inverse covariance estimation with the graphical lasso. *Biostatistics* 9(3), 432-441. doi:10.1093/biostatistics/kxm045. "a simple algorithm--the graphical lasso--that is remarkably fast".
- Longin, F. and Solnik, B. (2001). Extreme correlation of international equity markets. *Journal of Finance* 56(2), 649-676. doi:10.1111/0022-1082.00340. "Correlation increases in bear markets, but not in bull markets."
- Ang, A. and Chen, J. (2002). Asymmetric correlations of equity portfolios. *Journal of Financial Economics* 63(3), 443-494. doi:10.1016/S0304-405X(02)00068-5. Bibliography confirmed; abstract NOT confirmed at source.
- Burns, P., Engle, R. F. and Mezrich, J. J. (1998). Correlations and volatilities of asynchronous data. *Journal of Derivatives* 5(4), 7-18. doi:10.3905/jod.1998.408000. "the returns appear to have predictability and correlations are understated."
- Marti, G., Nielsen, F., Bińkowski, M. and Donnat, P. (2021). A review of two decades of correlations, hierarchies, networks and clustering in financial markets. In *Progress in Information Geometry*, Springer, 245-274. doi:10.1007/978-3-030-65459-7_10 (arXiv 1703.00485).

## Selection by factors, market portfolio, conditioning (checked 2026-11-13; RePEc, Crossref, OpenAlex)

- Jolliffe, I. T. (1972). Discarding variables in a principal component analysis. I: Artificial data. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series C (Applied Statistics)* 21(2), 160-173. doi:10.2307/2346488. Abstract: "examines some of the possible methods for deciding which variables to reject"; several "discard precisely those variables known to be 'redundant'". The largest-loading rule per component is NOT confirmed (full text not reached). Part II: Applied Statistics 22(1) (1973), doi:10.2307/2346300.
- McCabe, G. P. (1984). Principal variables. *Technometrics* 26(2), 137-144. doi:10.1080/00401706.1984.10487939. Subset of original variables keeping "as much information as possible" (abstract via OpenAlex); the specific criteria NOT confirmed.
- Doeswijk, R., Lam, T. and Swinkels, L. (2014). The global multi-asset market portfolio, 1959-2012. *Financial Analysts Journal* 70(2), 26-41. doi:10.2469/faj.v70.n2.1. Market-cap weights of the invested global multi-asset portfolio (abstract as summary, no verbatim quote). Update: Doeswijk, Lam and Swinkels (2020), Historical returns of the market portfolio, *Review of Asset Pricing Studies* 10(3), 521-567, doi:10.1093/rapstu/raz010; data published by the authors.
- Forbes, K. J. and Rigobon, R. (2002). No contagion, only interdependence: measuring stock market comovements. *Journal of Finance* 57(5), 2223-2261. doi:10.1111/0022-1082.00494. "Heteroskedasticity biases tests for contagion"; after adjustment virtually no rise in correlations in the 1997, 1994, 1987 crises. Use: correlation depends on the variance of the conditioning variable; their high-volatility case biases it up, our selection of SPY's worst weeks truncates SPY and biases it down. Same mechanism, opposite direction; say so. (Boyer, Gibson and Loretan, 1997, on truncation: NOT yet checked.)
